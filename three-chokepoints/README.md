## Structure (v2)
About 29 shots, mostly 1.5-5 s, all hard cuts: cold open on real footage, a world globe with shipping traffic, a montage of NASA photos ("narrow gaps"), and then one chapter each for 01 Hormuz, 02 Bab el-Mandeb (with the detour around Africa) and 03 Malacca. The finale shows 3D arcs from the Gulf to Asia and Europe.

## Visual system (from the cheat sheet)
- **Routes**:
  - R1 ship-traffic swarm: thousands of moving ships as dots with short tails, with separated lanes in each direction. Ships turn red and stop when a strait is blocked.
  - R2 volume ribbons, with a flowing highlight, for oil flows.
  - R5 lifted 3D arcs, each with a ground shadow, in the finale.
- **Type**:
  - T1 mask slide-up for all text.
  - T4 odometer rolling digits for numbers.
  - T5 location lower-thirds: amber rule, slide-up name, typed coordinates.
- **Data**:
  - D1 numbers pinned to the place they describe, using a leader line (39 km, 26 km, 1/5, +15 days, 2.7 km, 80%).
  - D2 full-screen number slams over dimmed footage (20,000,000; 8.7M to 4.0M, −54%; 23.2M vs 20.9M).
- No boxed data cards, no orange route lines, no whooshes.
- Globe: procedural cloud layer, vignette and film grain. Fonts: Barlow Condensed, IBM Plex Sans and IBM Plex Mono (SIL OFL).

## Facts and sources
- Hormuz: ~20 M bbl/day in 2024, ~20% of global petroleum liquids consumption (U.S. EIA). Narrowest ~39 km (measured from elevation data).
- Bab el-Mandeb: 8.7 M bbl/day (2023) to 4.0 M bbl/day (Jan-Aug 2024) after attacks from Nov 2023; Cape route ~15 days longer (U.S. EIA, Vortexa data). Narrowest ~26 km.
- Malacca: Phillips Channel ~2.7 km (EIA); 102,500+ ships in 2025 (Marine Department Malaysia, via news reports); 23.2 vs Hormuz 20.9 M bbl/day, H1 2025 (EIA, via news reports); ~80% of China's oil imports (widely cited estimate).
